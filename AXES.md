# Axes

**A standing table of how each story is built, so that "does this new story repeat an old one" has the same answer whoever asks.** The first panel audit ran two critics over the same pair of stories and one counted three shared axes where the other counted one (CALIBRATION.md § The panel against six stories). The comparison was judgement applied to prose, and judgement on prose does not repeat. This replaces it with values from a closed vocabulary, compared by identity.

**This is a spoiler file.** Every row says, in categories, how a story climaxes and ends. Rob does not open it. The sheet critic and the tabulating agents do.

## How it is used

- **The sheet critic fills six values for the new sheet from the vocabulary below and compares them with the table.** The six axes are DOMAIN, OBJECT, RELATIONSHIP, CLIMAX, ENDING and PRACTICE with PRESSURE, which count together as one axis. An axis is shared with a story when the values are identical, and practice-and-pressure is shared only when both match. For each axis the critic names the nearest story and the value they share.
- **Three or more shared axes with one story is a send-back.** Of the 276 pairs among the first 24 stories, two pairs share three axes, 18 share two, and none share four, so three is as close as the closest pairs already in the corpus. One of the two is 号令 with 三番の乾燥機, on object, relationship and ending; 号令 shipped before this rule and would have drawn a send-back on it.
- **The critic does not judge similarity from the other stories' prose.** If no value fits a new sheet, it proposes a new value by name rather than using OTHER. OTHER is a flag that the vocabulary has a gap.
- **When a story ships, add its row.** A tabulator fills it from the sheet and the afterword, a second one fills it independently, and a third settles any disagreement, which is how this table was built. It is the fifth place a story lands (AUTHORING.md § Where a story lands).
- **A new value goes in with a one-line definition that says how to tell it from its neighbours.** Re-tabulate nothing unless the new definition takes cells from an existing value.

## What the axes mean

- **DOMAIN.** The kind of place, trade or institution the story happens inside. This is the narrow-domain rule's own subject: the thing that recycles the vocabulary. It is not the plot.
- **OBJECT.** The kind of concrete thing the plot turns on, or NONE. It is the object's role in the plot and not its appearance: a lent umbrella and a lent book are the same value if the lending is what matters.
- **RELATIONSHIP.** The central pair, by kind of tie. Kin take a kin value before any role value.
- **CLIMAX.** What the climax scene does, as a mechanism: how a fact comes out, a choice is made or a cost is paid. It is not what the climax is about.
- **ENDING.** How the last page lands, judged first by the form of the final sentence and then by its image.
- **PRACTICE.** The named custom, institution or procedure the story runs on. It is the thing the story would lose if moved to a place without it, not the occasion that frames the story.
- **PRESSURE.** The social force that keeps the central need unsaid. Choose the one whose removal would let the need be spoken.

## Tie-breaks

These come from the cells the first two tabulators disagreed on. Each is a proposal until a second tabulation, of new stories, shows that it reduces disagreement.

- **CLIMAX.** Pick the beat where the central fact comes out or the central choice is made, not the beat that follows it. DEDUCTION only when the protagonist finds no new fact in the scene; if they see or handle one, it is HIDDEN_FACT_SEEN.
- **OBJECT.** If the plot turns on what the record says, RECORD_OR_REGISTER. If it turns on where the item is or who has it, GOODS_IN_TRANSFER.
- **ENDING.** Judge by the final sentence's form first: a quote is LINE_OF_SPEECH or QUESTION_LEFT_OPEN, and an image is read only when the last sentence is narration.
- **PRACTICE.** A calendar date is an occasion, and the occasion is not the practice. Choose the custom that does the work.
- **PRESSURE.** 遠慮 when the character withholds a request out of regard for the other. 察し when they expect to be understood unasked. 意地 when no audience is needed for the refusal. 面子 only when an audience is.

## How this table was built

On 2026-10-25, from the stored sheets, afterwords and blurbs, not from the stories themselves. Two tabulators worked independently from the same vocabulary and agreed on 138 of 154 cells, 90%: DOMAIN 22 of 22, OBJECT 21, RELATIONSHIP 22, CLIMAX 18, ENDING 20, PRACTICE 19, PRESSURE 16. A third reader settled the 16 disputed cells, taking tabulator A's value on six, B's on eight, and flagging two as ties. Two stories that reached `main` while the table was being built, 号令 and 墨の量, were added the same day by the same method. The vocabulary fit both without a single OTHER, the two tabulators agreed on 13 of 14 cells, and the one dispute, on PRESSURE, went to tabulator A. Across all 24 stories that is 151 agreed cells of 168, 90%. **PRESSURE is the least reliable axis, so a match on it deserves the least weight.** The Conf column is L where a story has no stored sheet or several values were judgement calls, and no row is H, because nothing here read a story. Both late additions are L: each tabulator rated them low on its own, though their sheets are of the current format, so the vocabulary's first test on stories it was not built from is a weaker result than the agreement figure alone suggests.

舌切り雀 was added on 2026-10-25 by the same method, after it had shipped without the panel. Two tabulators agreed on 6 of 7 cells; the one dispute, CLIMAX (UNEXPLAINED_EVENT against HIDDEN_FACT_SEEN), went to a third reader, who took UNEXPLAINED_EVENT because nothing in the scene is a fact the protagonist finds and uses. All three said TRAVEL_AND_DEPARTURE was only the nearest PRACTICE for a host seeing a guest off with a gift, and the third judged it acceptable, so no value was added; a second story built on hospitality and gifts would be the reason to add one. The closest existing stories share two axes with it, 時計の音 and 十五日の質札, below the three that make a send-back.

## The vocabulary

### 1. DOMAIN
- HOME_HOUSEHOLD: the story happens inside a private dwelling and the family life or family property in it, including clearing, keeping or taking over a dwelling.
- OWNER_RUN_SHOP: a small shop run by its owner that sells, lends, lends against a pledge, or repairs goods across a counter; the tie to customers is a sale or a loan of goods.
- SERVICE_PREMISES: a place where customers are physically present to receive a repeated cleaning, grooming, feeding or similar service on the provider's schedule or machines; the tie is the repeat visit, not the goods.
- SKILLED_TRADE: a hand craft or trade practised out of customers' sight or on call at their sites, defined by skill, tools and lineage rather than by a counter.
- PUBLIC_INSTITUTION: an institution run for the public without a sale (library, school, hospital, registry, police, office), where its rules and staff roles drive events.
- TRANSIT: a vehicle, station or route in ordinary use, where people are in motion between one place and another.
- PUBLIC_COMMONS: an open shared outdoor or street space with no owner or staff, where people meet by habit or chance.
- RESIDENTS_COLLECTIVE: the organised life of a street, block or building as a group, with rotating duties, shared notices or shared upkeep.
- FARM_OR_VILLAGE_LAND: agricultural land and the village economy around it, with tenancy, harvest and neighbours' dependence on weather and crops.
- REMOTE_TERRAIN: mountain, forest, coast, or a lodge or road beyond ordinary settlement, where distance and weather shape what people can do.
- HIRED_PROFESSIONAL: a private professional engaged by a client for a task and a fee (search, advice, errand); the job brief is the frame.
- COURT_OR_GARRISON: a ruler's household, castle, army or other seat of command, where orders and rank decide events.
- OTHER: none of the above fits; use rarely and name the missing kind of place.

### 2. OBJECT
- KEEPSAKE: a personal possession valued for the person who owned, made or used it, kept or found after that person's death or departure, with no further function in the plot.
- WRITTEN_MESSAGE: a text addressed to someone and passed along to reach them, whose delivery, wording or reply is the plot.
- RECORD_OR_REGISTER: a register, ticket, number, stamp, card or notebook that fixes who owes, owns, has done or has been where.
- DEPICTION: a drawn, painted or photographic image of a place, route or person that stands in for the real thing.
- ACCESS_DEVICE: a device that controls who may enter, leave or open a space, and who holds it.
- TOOL_OF_SKILL: an implement, instrument or game set whose skilled handling or upkeep is the plot.
- FOOD_OR_VESSEL: prepared food or drink, its taste or quantity, or the container it travels in.
- GOODS_IN_TRANSFER: ordinary possessions whose lending, swapping, pledging, selling or registering to a new owner is the plot, as things rather than as records.
- INSTALLED_FIXTURE: equipment fixed to a building or used by many (an appliance, boiler, window fitting, large bell) whose working or failure is the plot.
- LIVING_THING: a living creature or a crop, and its whereabouts, growth or fate.
- TRACE_OR_SIGNAL: a mark, residue or signal left by someone's presence or act, read by others as evidence of what was done.
- LAND_OR_PREMISES: a bounded piece of ground or room that is kept aside, lent, rented or left untouched.
- NONE: no single concrete thing carries the plot.
- OTHER: a concrete thing that fits none of the above; use rarely and name it by kind.

### 3. RELATIONSHIP
- PARENT_AND_CHILD: a parent and their child, any age, both active in the present story; kin always take a kin value before any role value.
- GRANDPARENT_AND_GRANDCHILD: a grandparent or older forebear and a grandchild or descendant; the elder may be dying, dead or remembered.
- SPOUSES_OR_PARTNERS: a married or committed pair.
- SIBLINGS_OR_OTHER_KIN: brothers, sisters, cousins, in-laws or other kin not covered above.
- MENTOR_AND_SUCCESSOR: a non-kin holder of a trade, post or craft and the younger person who learns it or is to inherit it; the tie is the passing on of work.
- PROVIDER_AND_CUSTOMER: a seller or service-giver and a customer, non-kin; the tie is trade.
- CUSTODIAN_AND_PATRON: a staff member of a public institution and a member of the public it serves without a sale; the tie is the rule and the duty of care.
- NEIGHBOURS: people sharing a street, block or building whose tie is proximity, with standing friction or unspoken distance.
- STRANGERS_BY_CHANCE: people with no prior tie thrown together briefly, by one night, one exchange or one misdirected contact.
- ROUTINE_ACQUAINTANCES: people with no tie outside a repeated fixed-time meeting, who know each other only through that routine.
- SUCCESSOR_AND_ABSENT_PREDECESSOR: a living person who occupies the place, post or secret of someone dead, gone or never met, where the tie runs only through what that person left and they are not kin.
- FRIENDS_OR_PEERS: equals who chose each other and share a history.
- OTHER: a central pair that fits none of the above; use rarely and name the tie.

### 4. CLIMAX
- HIDDEN_FACT_SEEN: the protagonist comes upon a concealed fact with their own eyes or hands (walking in, finding, handling) and nobody tells them.
- HIDDEN_FACT_SAID: another character states the concealed fact aloud in the scene, in plain words or a single line.
- RECORD_READ_OUT: a written record, mark, label or drawing is read or decoded in the scene and its true meaning reverses what everyone assumed, including the protagonist's own record read out against them.
- DEDUCTION: the protagonist alone joins facts already shown to the reader into the missing conclusion, and nobody supplies it.
- CONFESSION: a character volunteers aloud something they owe, did or withheld, without being cornered; the listener may be a stranger or no one.
- REFUSAL_OVERTURNED: a refusal, ruling or decision the protagonist held throughout is reversed by them in the scene (a request is finally made, a demand given up, a no turned to yes).
- RULE_UPHELD_AT_COST: the protagonist keeps a rule or refusal against what someone asks, and keeping it costs them what they wanted; the rule is shown to protect something.
- CONCEALMENT_CHOSEN: the protagonist lies, erases evidence or stays silent on purpose so that another can keep something; the cover-up is the act.
- HANDOVER_OR_TAKEOVER: a tool, token, task, role or charge passes between people by a physical act without explanation.
- PRICE_PAID: the protagonist pays a concrete cost they choose (money, public exposure, a sacrifice) in a form the other side recognises, to settle a debt or make good; the cost is the scene.
- PHYSICAL_DEMONSTRATION: a physical act or event, performed or undergone (a removal, a test, a repair), proves a hidden function or truth by its effect, and no one explains it.
- UNEXPLAINED_EVENT: an uncanny or impossible fact stands in the scene and no character explains it; the scene's force is what it implies.
- OTHER: a mechanism that fits none of the above; use rarely and name the mechanism.

### 5. ENDING
- ACT_OF_CARE: the last beat is the protagonist doing a small practical task for another person or for what is at stake, narrated without comment.
- ROUTINE_RESUMES: the ordinary pattern of the story's world starts again with one altered detail, and people go on doing what they always did.
- OBJECT_PLACED: the last beat is a thing put down, left, kept or put in its place, and the placement is the statement.
- LINE_OF_SPEECH: the last words are a quoted line spoken by a character, or a short quoted exchange, that is not a question.
- QUESTION_LEFT_OPEN: the story ends on a question, spoken or implied, with no answer given.
- NARRATED_CORRECTION: the last sentence is the narrator's own statement, outside quotation marks, correcting a belief, claim or earlier sentence.
- ABSENCE_IMAGE: the last image is a lack: an empty place, a vanished trace, a signal that has stopped.
- ECHO_OF_OPENING: the last image repeats the opening scene almost exactly, changed only in what it now means.
- WRITTEN_ENTRY_OR_MESSAGE: the last beat is something written, stamped, signed or posted.
- SENSORY_MOMENT: the last beat is a bodily perception held without comment (a sound, warmth or colour perceived).
- DEFLATED_FACT: the story closes on a plain, unredeemed practical fact (a cost, a chill, a chore left) that undercuts the resolution.
- OTHER: an ending that fits none of the above; use rarely and name how it lands.

### 6. PRACTICE_PRESSURE
Record each story as one PRACTICE plus one PRESSURE, chosen from the two lists below.

#### PRACTICE (the custom, institution or procedure the story runs on)
- ESTATE_CLEARING_AND_MOURNING: clearing, keeping or discarding a dead person's belongings or dwelling, and the customs of leaving a dead person's place as it was.
- HANDED_DOWN_RULE_OR_PACT: an instruction, taboo, pact or last request kept after its giver or its reason is gone.
- APPRENTICESHIP_AND_SUCCESSION: the passing of a trade, post or craft from an older holder to a younger one, with its unwritten standards.
- LOAN_PLEDGE_AND_RETURN: lending, borrowing or pledging goods with a due date or an expectation of return, and the custom of returning them.
- REGISTRY_AND_OFFICIAL_RULE: a register, registration, licence or enforced public rule that fixes who owns what, with stated consequences.
- CORRESPONDENCE_AND_NOTICE: letters, circulated or posted notices and replies, as the channel through which people address each other.
- REGULAR_PATRONAGE: a customer returning to the same shop, chair or counter, with the provider reading the customer by habit.
- STANDING_APPOINTMENT: two people keeping a fixed-time meeting for a shared ritual such as a game or a visit, outside any sale.
- HOUSEHOLD_STEWARDSHIP: one household member feeding, minding the money of, locking up for or otherwise managing another's daily needs.
- TRAVEL_AND_DEPARTURE: the customs of leaving a place, such as a last journey, a final train, or a trip made on another's behalf.
- SEASONAL_OR_YEAR_END_SETTLEMENT: closing a business, season or account at a calendar date, including dues paid at harvest.
- PAID_COMMISSION: hiring a professional for a task and a fee, with the brief and the report as the frame.
- OTHER: a custom or procedure that fits none of the above; use rarely and name it.

#### PRESSURE (the social force that keeps the central need unsaid; choose the one whose removal would let the need be spoken)
- 遠慮: holding back a request or complaint so as not to burden the other; the one withholding is protecting the other from a demand.
- 義理: an obligation owed by role, promise or inherited charge to a person or to the memory of one, kept regardless of affection.
- 世間体: fear of how neighbours or the public will see the person or the family.
- 恩 and お返し: an unrepaid favour that creates a debt and the pressure to repay in kind, so that speaking the need would admit the debt.
- 察し: the expectation that the other will read the need without being told, so saying it would shame both.
- 建前: keeping to the official line, rule, title or formula in place of the real feeling, so that role-speech does the talking.
- 迷惑: fear of disturbing others' peace, time or space, or of being accused of doing so; a complaint about nuisance rather than a withheld request.
- 面子: fear of being seen by the other person to fail, be corrected or be surpassed; the audience is the other party or witnesses.
- 先輩・後輩: seniority order, in which a junior cannot ask or correct a senior and a senior cannot ask a junior.
- 意地: private pride that refuses to owe, ask or be helped even when no one is watching (a close relative of 面子, which needs an audience).
- none: nothing social keeps the need unsaid; the barrier is only the character's own fear or ignorance.
