# 📚 Known-Word Stories

**This file is a build input.** `indexmd.py` reads the two `##` sections below and nothing else: `## Summaries` feeds each card's blurb and kana reading on the contents page, `## Afterwords` is folded into the reader and opened on the last page. One bullet per story in each, `- **｜題名《だいめい》** — text`, keyed on the title exactly as `corpus.json` spells it.

A story with no Summaries bullet still ships a card — `index.py` warns on stderr and leaves the blurb blank rather than stopping a rebuild that has already spent a minute per story in Ichiran. A missing Afterwords bullet is not checked at all.

Everything else that used to sit here was documentation of the reader, the build and the corpus, kept in parallel with the three files that own those subjects and wrong in all three by 2026-09-16: it described 傍点 as coloured underlines two designs out of date, counted five stories when there were eight, and quoted a known-set size 5 lemmas stale. Two-thirds of a build input, loaded as context beside the files it contradicted. It was deleted rather than corrected — see [README.md](../README.md) for the reader and the build, [AUTHORING.md](../AUTHORING.md) for the craft spec and the new-word rules, and [CALIBRATION.md](../CALIBRATION.md) for where the thresholds came from, which is where the two false claims this file used to record have gone.

## Afterwords

Full spoilers. The reader keeps these folded away until the last page, and `index.py` does not put them on the contents page.

They exist because of a specific mistake. The 2026-09-08 prose pass cut the theme-announcing line from all five stories on the grounds that a narrator explaining the point is weaker than a scene that earns it. That is right for a native reader and wrong here: it removed the only handhold and left inference chains running thirty-plus sentences, in a reader that shows one sentence at a time with the English underneath. The fix is not to put ｜郷愁《きょうしゅう》というのは… back into the Japanese. It is to say it in English, afterwards, once the reading is done.

- **｜時計の音《とけいのおと》** — The grandfather's silence was never rejection, and three things say so without any of them saying it. The shop owner: 「｜何《なに》も｜買《か》わないで、｜店《みせ》の｜時計《とけい》の｜音《おと》を｜聞《き》いていた」. The letter: 「だから、｜直《なお》さなかった」. And the one time he was angry, 「｜静《しず》かにしろ。｜音《おと》が｜聞《き》こえない」. Read forward, that is a man telling a child to go away. Read back from the letter, it is a man asking the child to listen with him and failing to say so. The last page is the narrator finally doing what was asked: ｜時計《とけい》を｜耳《みみ》に｜当《あ》てて、｜目《め》を｜閉《と》じた. He hears it, and the story stops there rather than explaining it. The repair is where the watch goes afterwards — ｜部品《ぶひん》の｜箱《はこ》の｜隣《となり》ではなく、｜机《つくえ》の｜上《うえ》に｜置《お》いた — on the workbench, not in with the drawer of sorted parts. - **｜塩の卵焼き《しおのたまごやき》** — The father thinks the ｜弁当箱《べんとうばこ》 is a test he is passing. It comes home empty and washed every evening, and he reads that as his silent daughter growing up and as proof he can run the house alone. Everything that undoes him is on the page first. ｜千秋《ちあき》's ｜卵焼《たまごや》き have always been sweet and ｜葵《あおい》 likes them sweet; he makes his salted, the way his mother did, and settles it on the phone: 「｜卵焼《たまごや》きは｜塩《しお》だ。もう｜決《き》めた」. ｜葵《あおい》 has never washed her own glass. The hospital is next door to the school, and she comes home after seven, 「｜友達《ともだち》と」. So the clean box was never her doing. She ate the rest of the lunch at noon and carried the ｜卵焼《たまごや》き next door, and her mother ate them and washed the box so that nothing came home and nobody had to tell him. Her silence was not coldness. It was a thirteen-year-old who could not say she did not like it to a father trying that hard. What he gives up in the hospital room is the idea that he was doing any of this alone, and the line where he gives it up is a question: 「｜砂糖《さとう》は、どのくらい｜入《い》れるんだ」. It is the first time in the story he asks anyone how to do something. The last page answers it without anyone saying so. The box comes home empty and, for the first time, dirty, and he washes it himself.
- **｜迷子の手紙《まいごのてがみ》** — Two stories, and the narrator only notices one. The visible one is the son's: a letter to a mother who died before it arrived, answered by a stranger, closed by a reply signed with a granddaughter's name the mother never knew. The invisible one is hers. The neighbour described ｜青山雪《あおやまゆき》 as ｜毎日《まいにち》｜同《おな》じ｜時間《じかん》に｜買《か》い｜物《もの》へ｜出《で》かけて、｜誰《だれ》とも｜話《はな》さなかった. On the last page the narrator's own shopping hour has become the same every day, and the neighbour says 「｜松田《まつだ》さんも｜静《しず》かな｜人《ひと》ね」 — the same word she used for the dead woman. She moved into more than the house. Her one reach outward is the word ｜丁寧《ていねい》, which the neighbour never used and she added anyway: an invented kindness, for someone she will never meet.
- **｜鍵とチェーン《かぎとチェーン》** — A son spends a morning looking for a key and finds every reason he has been saving up to be afraid. Each clue is on the page before he looks at it: the chain left off, the wet newspaper, the grey fingertips, his own old jacket on his father's back. He files every one as decline, because decline is what the back page of his notebook was made to catch. The same facts describe a man of seventy-one getting up at four to deliver newspapers in the rain, earning money his son cannot count, for a ring his wife never got when they married. The chain is the hinge. The son says it aloud as a fact about doors, 「チェーンは、外からは掛けられない」, and it is the first true thing he has said about his father all morning. The father answers with an envelope and one line about the notebook. The son answers by handing over the key and keeping none himself, which is the end of managing him. The chain he leaves hanging that night is for someone who will be out again before dawn.
- **｜終電《しゅうでん》** — About the conditions under which a person can finally say a true thing. He has told nobody that he quit, and the reason is given in the second page: ｜話《はな》す｜相手《あいて》がいないからだ. He says it to her — 「｜今日《きょう》までは」 — precisely because she boards the morning bullet train and he will never see her again. Her 「｜知《し》らない｜人《ひと》が｜多《おお》いほど、｜楽《らく》なんです」 is her reason for going to ｜東京《とうきょう》 and his reason for talking to her, and neither of them notices the overlap. She never asks what happened to him, and the narration says outright that this was the best thing about the night. They are carrying the same thing in different bags: hers holds her dead mother's ｜茶碗《ちゃわん》, wrapped in paper, everything else thrown away, and his holds the company papers he still cannot throw away. Her 「もう｜帰《かえ》る｜家《いえ》がないんです」 is left standing — he cannot answer it, and the story refuses to give him a line that would. What he does instead is the last page: the papers come out onto the desk, he turns the top sheet over, and the blank back is where he writes the names of towns he has never been to. The ｜網棚《あみだな》 over their heads is empty from the first exchange to the last line, because neither of them will put anything down. **A reading note:** 38 of the 39 quoted lines carry no attribution. Who is speaking comes from register alone — he asks, at length and over-politely, and she answers short. The narration hands you that key just before the longest exchange: ｜彼女《かのじょ》の｜返事《へんじ》はいつも｜短《みじか》い。｜私《わたし》の｜質問《しつもん》は、どうしても｜長《なが》くなる。
- **｜猫を探す探偵《ねこをさがすたんてい》** — The case closes on page 21 and the story runs to thirty, which is the design: finding the cat, the boy and the reason takes five days, and the rest is deciding what to do about it. Everything the reader needs is in plain view early. The boxes outside a house on page 8 are a family packing to move, and the detective walks straight past them. The boy names the white ear-tip without once looking at the photograph — 「その｜猫《ねこ》、｜耳《みみ》の｜先《さき》が｜白《しろ》いよ」 — and the line the detective has already heard twice is that only an owner remembers the tip of an ear. The payoff is him putting those two facts together on page 21, one page after the boy says 「｜来週《らいしゅう》、｜引《ひ》っ｜越《こ》すんだ」. What the story is actually about is the second plant, and it is about the detective rather than the boy. He does not open the warehouse door when he could — ｜大人《おとな》が｜扉《とびら》を｜開《あ》ければ、その｜子《こ》は｜次《つぎ》の｜場所《ばしょ》を｜探《さが》すだけだ — and he promises only the one thing he can keep, 「｜君《きみ》が｜盗《ぬす》んだとは｜言《い》わない」, while fully intending to tell ｜松田《まつだ》 everything. That is not kindness and the story never calls it kindness. It is a man who borrowed a book at twelve, never gave it back, can no longer remember whose it was, and still has it on his office shelf. ｜松田《まつだ》 then gives up something she is entitled to keep: she says 「｜私《わたし》の｜猫《ねこ》です」 once and never says it again. The old photograph on her shelf, the cat beside a man the detective does not ask about, is the only evidence of what the animal is worth to her, and she does not explain that either. Three people hand something over and none of them announce it. The last page is deliberately flat — the office is cold, the book is exactly where it has always been, and half a cat-finding fee does not cover the rent.
- **｜父の鋏《ちちのはさみ》** — The son spends the story trying to take the chair, and he reads every piece of evidence wrong. ｜佐野《さの》's 「っ」 in the first scene sounds to him like age, the silent Sunday nights like tiredness, and his father alone at night with a thumb on the blade like a man mourning his skill. What was actually failing was the one job the son was made to do as a boy and has refused ever since: sharpening. The father's hands can no longer hold a blade steady on the stone, and he would rather cut with dull scissors than ask. ｜佐野《さの》 would rather be hurt every month than say so, because saying so would end it, and his 「先月も、その前の月も痛かったぞ」 is the whole reveal, said to the father in the mirror and not to the son. The turn is one sentence, 私は自分の鋏を台に置いて、父の手から鋏を取った: he puts down the scissors he wanted to cut with and goes back to the stone he resented. He never cuts ｜佐野《さの》's hair. Nobody thanks anybody. 「寝かせすぎだ」 and 「もう少し立てろ」 are the same two lines the father said to him at ten, and now they are the nearest thing to approval the son will get. ｜佐野《さの》 looks him in the eye for the first time to say 「来月も頼むぞ」, and he means the sharpening. The last page is the father behind him on a Sunday night with a newspaper he never turns.
- **｜城の鐘《しろのかね》** — The master's crime is the plot; the inheritance is the story. Forty years earlier he cast the war bell thin at the top and thick at the bottom so it would crack under a hard strike, and told no one until he was dying. He calls it guilt: 「これは｜罪《つみ》だ」, 「わしは｜裏切《うらぎ》り｜者《もの》だ」. The apprentice takes the same act onto himself on the last page and then refuses the word for it — ｜私《わたし》は｜師匠《ししょう》の｜罪《つみ》を、｜自分《じぶん》のものとして｜引《ひ》き｜受《う》けた。けれど、｜罪《つみ》だとはもう｜思《おも》っていない — which is the one thing in the story that changes. Everything hangs on a distinction stated once, in dialogue on page 12: あの｜鐘《かね》は、｜強《つよ》く｜打《う》てば｜必《かなら》ず｜割《わ》れる。｜弱《よわ》く｜打《う》てば、｜良《よ》い｜音《おと》がする. Every scene after it is somebody deciding how hard to strike, and the two men who could have stopped it both decline to ask the question that would have told them. The captain asks 「｜鐘《かね》は｜打《う》てる｜状態《じょうたい》か」 when he could have asked whether it would crack; when he finally does ask that, he has come down to the cell without a key on him. The closing image repeats the opening exactly: a twelve-year-old with no parents, striking a small bell hard, and not being stopped.

- **｜煙突の煙《えんとつのけむり》** — The owner has been heating the water past closing for three months, waiting for a regular who came late from the factory. That regular was her father, and nobody ever told him. She has not told him either, and she has spent the whole week clearing the flat without telling anyone in the neighbourhood she was there. The chimney is how these people used to pass news without speaking: as a child she knew her father was on his way home when the smoke went out, and it is the same smoke still going up an hour past the eleven o'clock on the notice that makes her ask. The answer is sitting in plain view from page 7: every ｜桶《おけ》 on the shelf is upended on its base, and one is left standing mouth-up, kept aside for a man who is not coming. When she finally tells him on the last night, the owner does not speak at all. He climbs down from the ｜番台《ばんだい》, lifts that ｜桶《おけ》 off the shelf in both hands and fills it, which is the first thing in the story to be finished rather than kept waiting. She sets her father's worn-down soap on the ｜番台《ばんだい》. Two pages later the ｜桶《おけ》 has been turned over and stacked in with all the others, and nothing is said about that either. The closing image is the smoke gone before ten, an hour earlier than the notice allows, seen from the window she watched it from as a child, and this time it means what it always meant.

- **｜行かなかった人の地図《いかなかったひとのちず》** — The map is not wrong. It is early. The bridge ｜梓《あずさ》 cannot find fell seventy-five years ago, the year of the great snow, five years before her grandfather was born, which means he never saw it either. He was not drawing the road. He was drawing his father's journey, exactly as it was told to him, and a told journey does not update. The small ｜印《しるし》 beside each landmark, which she put down to an old man's habit as a child and never once asked about, are counts: one for every time his father told him of a thing. Four beside the bridge, seven beside the village, twelve beside the split rock, sixty-three beside the tree at the end, which is why the tree is drawn far too large and why none of the distances are true. The map is drawn to the scale of what was remembered rather than what was walked, and that is how it fails at every particular and still arrives. The ｜主《ぬし》 on the ｜峠《とうげ》 asks the question the whole story is made of, and ｜梓《あずさ》's first answer is refused because it is her grandfather's sentence and not hers. The one thing on the paper he could not get right is the sea, a flat blue line, because the sea is the one thing his father had words for and no way to show him. It is also the only thing he ever wanted. He does not ask her about the tree when she comes home.

- **｜廊下の鏡《ろうかのかがみ》** — The mirror was never for looking into. The grandmother hung it at the turn of the hallway, set at an angle to the one window at the front of the house, so that the light would go around the corner into the back rooms that have no window of their own. She never said so. She said only that it was not to be moved, and she polished it far more often than a mirror anyone looked into would need, because dust costs light. Her daughter inherited the instruction without the reason, kept the angle, kept polishing, and treated both as a rule rather than as a mechanism, which is why she wrapped the glass herself at the roof repair and stood over the workmen until the nail went back in the same hole. Every clue is in plain view from the first page. It hangs where nobody dresses. It faces a window rather than a room. It is cleaned on bright days and left alone on dull ones. She is twice shown polishing it and never once shown looking at her own reflection, which is what the 一方で sentence on page nine is for. And the child never needs a lamp at the turn of the hallway, which she reports as a fact about her childhood rather than as a fact about the mirror. The turn is an event and not a realization: she lifts the mirror off the nail to wrap it, and the back of the hallway goes dark around her while she is standing in it holding the thing that was lighting it. She puts it back three times, and by the third she is counting years instead of checking. Thirty of them, none of it ever explained to anyone. The last line corrects a sentence the reader was handed on page ten in her parents' voices, 「おばあさんは、不思議なことを信じる人だから」, and it is a correction about a person rather than a statement about the story.

- **｜縁台の将棋《えんだいのしょうぎ》** — ｜広子《ひろこ》 is much the stronger player and has been resigning from winning positions for a year. A game she wins is a game that ends, and when it ends the narrator goes home. So every Sunday she plays to the point where she could finish it, looks at the finish, and says 「｜負《ま》けました」 instead, which buys a reset and another hour. The reader is given the mechanism on page three and told it is something else: ｜四十《よんじゅう》｜手《て》のあたりまで｜来《く》ると、それまで｜速《はや》かった｜手《て》が｜急《きゅう》に｜止《と》まってしまう — a hand that has been quick and then is not, which reads as a weak player running out of ideas and is in fact a strong one deciding. What the narrator never notices is that she is doing the identical thing from the other side. She buys the book and reads every night, and the narration is explicit that this is not because she likes the game: ｜長《なが》く｜指《さ》していたかったからだ. Both of them are working to keep the other on the bench, neither says so, and each believes the effort is one-sided. The irony is that the studying is what breaks it. She gets strong enough to read a board, and the turn is not a realization but an action — she goes round to ｜広子《ひろこ》's side of the ｜盤《ばん》 after she has gone, rebuilds the position, and finds ｜詰《つ》み sitting in it two moves deep. A year of Sundays reorganises itself in one line: ｜私《わたし》が｜強《つよ》くなるにつれて、｜止《と》まる｜場所《ばしょ》が｜遠《とお》くなっていただけだった. The stopping place had been moving because she had been moving it. Nothing is ever said out loud. What happens instead is that the next Sunday ｜広子《ひろこ》 moves first for the first time, plays the game all the way out, wins it, and asks for a second — which is the same request the resignations were making for a year, finally made in a form that does not require her to lose. The ｜頬《ほお》 are red at the end as they were in December, and the story says only that it is not the cold.
- **｜三番の乾燥機《さんばんのかんそうき》** — A man breaks something by fixing it, and then unfixes it by lying. The woman under the heater uses the one ｜乾燥機《かんそうき》 in the coin laundry that does not heat, washes very little, and runs the cold machine through twice, which is two hours. The narrator, new to the street and still annoyed at having carried his own wash home ｜生乾《なまがわ》き, works out only the first half of that: the machine is broken and nobody has said so. He tells the ｜主人《しゅじん》. A ｜貼《は》り｜紙《がみ》 goes up. The following week she reads it, takes ｜四番《よんばん》, and is out the door before nine. Nothing in the story says she has nowhere warm to be and he never asks, so what the reader is given instead is arithmetic, and it is all on the page from page six: one small bag against his week's pile, two cycles, two hours, against his own ｜一時間《いちじかん》と｜少《すこ》し. The thirty-minute week is where the subtraction becomes visible, and it becomes visible to the reader at the same moment it becomes visible to him. She does tell him, once, in the flattest possible form — 「｜空《す》いてるから」 — and then again more honestly two pages later when he presses: 「あなたには、ね」. Number two being faster is advice for somebody who wants to go home. She does not. The turn is one line, and it is an act rather than a realization. The ｜主人《しゅじん》, counting him a regular now, asks him to mind the place for the ｜業者《ぎょうしゃ》, and when the ｜業者《ぎょうしゃ》 asks 「どこが｜悪《わる》いんですか」 he opens the machine, looks inside, closes it, and says 「｜別《べつ》に、｜悪《わる》くないです」. The ｜貼《は》り｜紙《がみ》 comes down. The machine stays cold. He has told a lie about a physical fact in order to give somebody back two hours of heat, and the story does not say that is what he has done. The last page is a second ｜乾燥機《かんそうき》. She puts her bag in ｜三番《さんばん》 and sits down without looking at him, and he puts his own wash in as well, ｜乾《かわ》かないことを｜知《し》っていて、｜入《い》れた — which is the same sentence shape the story used for her on page fifteen, when he had only just noticed she was doing it. Forty minutes of laundry is going to take him two hours from now on. That is the only thing in the story that says he has changed, and the other is smaller: the ｜小銭《こぜに》 he did not have on his first night are ready in his bag on his last.
- **｜十五日の質札《じゅうごにちのしちふだ》** — A man cheats a ghost out of one day, is never caught, and tells her anyway. The narrator is closing the ｜質屋《しちや》 his father left him, and he wants one thing, stated on the first page and never hidden: the ｜蔵《くら》 empty by ｜大晦日《おおみそか》. What stands in the way is a customer who has come on the third of every month for longer than the current ledger runs, redeeming the same ｜櫛《くし》 and pawning it again for sums too small to be worth the paperwork. The story hands the reader the supernatural early and cheaply, because it is not the point: next door has been shut on the third for twenty years, he has never once seen her come in or leave, and the hand in the oldest book is neither his nor his father's. By the eleventh page the reader knows what she is. So does he, and he does nothing with it. What the reader is not given early is the thing on page two, because it is written as carelessness. He takes a blank ticket out of the drawer, tears it, writes 「使わず」 on it and puts it back, and the story explains why in the flattest possible terms: ｜番号《ばんごう》が｜飛《と》べば、｜記録《きろく》が｜正確《せいかく》でなくなるからだ. It is his father's rule, kept for forty years. The number is 二一四, and nothing marks it. The turn the story is actually built on is not supernatural at all. The record says the ｜締《し》め｜切《き》り is the fifteenth and the ticket in her hand says the sixteenth, one of them is a slip of the pen, and for the first time in forty years he does not correct it, because the fifteenth empties the ｜蔵《くら》 on schedule and the sixteenth costs him a day. He puts his whole case out loud to an empty shop, 「一日くらい」, and then sells the ｜櫛《くし》 to a dealer for less than she had been paying him every month. That is what the saved day was worth. She comes on the sixteenth with the money. He tells her the truth unprompted and completely, including the part nobody could ever have proved: 「そのほうが、こちらの｜都合《つごう》がよかったからです」. She answers はい to all of it. She is not angry and does not accuse him, and asks only to be allowed to look at the empty shelf, which is the worst thing available to her. The ｜質札《しちふだ》 she hands over is 二一四, and the torn one he voided is still in his drawer, whole, an arm's length away. He lays them side by side and looks from one to the other, and when he looks up she is gone. Nobody explains it, including him, and he tells nobody: 言ったところで、どうなるものでもない. He closes on schedule. He gets precisely the thing he wanted, one day early, and the last page is what it cost. The pale place on the shelf where the box stood will not sweep out. On ｜元旦《がんたん》 the door moves in the wind and he says 「いらっしゃいませ」 to an empty ｜土間《どま》 before he can stop himself. The ticket is still in his notebook, and the story does not say which of the two it is.
- **｜回覧板の返事《かいらんばんのへんじ》** — A man is corrected by his own handwriting. ｜山本《やまもと》 reads every sign of a learner as an insult: the replies in hiragana with a space between each word, the question about what you do at a drill, the ｜訓練《くんれん》 copied out of his own note, the thank-you he takes for sarcasm. Each one is the plainest thing it could be. His angriest notes, long and exact and full of kanji, were the best lessons she had, and she copied every one into a notebook with the kana written over it. At the door she asks him how to read the first word of his letter, and he has to say ｜迷惑《めいわく》 aloud to the person it was written about. She knows the word. In that doorway he is handed everything he asked for, the two days and an end to the notes, and he gives the two days back with 「｜三日《みっか》でいい」, because the notes were what he had wanted. His wife had known since summer and waited for him to ask. The plant that pays off last is the signature. He signed every note ｜班長《はんちょう》 and never his name, because the title was the only way he knew how to talk to anyone. On the last notice he writes 山本 with やまもと above it, the same kana he has started putting over every kanji for her, and the answer comes back addressed to him by name and signed with hers.
- **｜スープを残す客《すーぷをのこすきゃく》** — The reader is ahead of ｜松田《まつだ》 from the first page. A man who drinks his water first, swallows a white packet after eating, writes something in a notebook and turns up at the hospital needs no explaining, and half a bowl means exactly what it looks like. ｜松田《まつだ》 reads every one of those as a verdict on his broth, because for thirty years an empty bowl has been the only praise he knows how to hear. So he fixes the broth, and every fix aims at the wrong problem and lands on someone else: double bones that sweat the student, a cooled bowl served to the wrong man, a private pot that thins until his own apprentice calls it hot water. When the empty bowl finally comes, it is the one bowl that does not count. 「これは、お湯だ。数に入らん」 is the whole reveal, and the notebook carries the rest: the doctor sets the man's salt, and on every date he has spent all of it at this counter, half a bowl at a time. The half he was fighting was the most anyone had ever paid him. What ｜松田《まつだ》 gives up is the bowl to the bottom, and nobody says so; he pours the half away and does not look at the line. The nickname he kept in his head ends up on the wall in ｜小林《こばやし》's handwriting, on the one bowl its namesake refuses to order.
- **｜狐に貸した隅《きつねにかしたすみ》** — The same four words are spoken twice, sixty years apart, and they are only true the second time. In a cold year ハル's grandmother, who lends but will not borrow and sends the neighbour's rice back on the first page, lends the worst corner of her rented ｜田《た》んぼ to a ｜狐《きつね》 in a young man's shape, to be repaid in rice at harvest. The corner turns gold while the rest stays thin, the rent is due the day after harvest, and the nine-year-old cuts the fox's rice by moonlight. On the drying line behind the house it has turned to leaves, exactly as ｜松吉《まつきち》さん warned, though the story wrote his warning as envy because he was looking at her grandmother's field while he gave it. When the fox comes with the promised sack, her grandmother says 「わしが｜刈《か》った」. He looks past her at the child, leaves the rice anyway, and goes on borrowing the corner 「｜刈《か》った｜人《ひと》が、｜返《かえ》しに｜来《く》るまで」. That clause is the whole debt: sixty years of leaving the corner standing never paid it, because it was never asking for the corner. The teller tells it all in the third person, about some girl called ハル, and calls the corner lent to the sparrows. She tells it tonight because tomorrow her grandson will cut that corner and become the one who cut it, so she does for him what her grandmother did for her, with the same words, and this time they are true. The fox never answers. The last line is her own warning about the stone, handed back to her by the boy who is now carrying the light.
- **｜黄色い傘《きいろいかさ》** — A woman is convicted by her own ledger. The bookseller keeps exact accounts, and what they are really for is that she never owes anyone anything. When her green umbrellas stop coming back and yellow ones arrive in their place, she reads every clue as theft: the yellow umbrellas are cheap ones from the station kiosk, the baker's shout of 「傘！傘！」 across the street is mockery, and the baker walking towards her shop under a green umbrella with another under his arm is a man coming to gloat. The reader is meant to see from the second page what she has complained about for ten years, which is that his sign is yellow. Nobody in the neighbourhood knew there were two lending systems; they borrowed from whichever shop was nearer and returned to whichever shop they passed. He worked it out at once, called to her, came to her door, and when she shut it and locked it he dried her umbrellas, tagged each one 本屋, and waited for her to come in. She, meanwhile, lent his umbrellas out as the thief's repayment and wrote every one down. So the precision that was supposed to prove her right is what proves her wrong, and she has to read it aloud, 黄色1 to 黄色8, in front of his queue. She gets back every green umbrella she wanted and walks out owing a man she has refused to speak to for a decade. The あんパン is the first instalment. His daily 「たまにはパン、買いに来てよ！」 gets its answer as 「やっと来た！」, and she does not apologise or explain; buying his bread is the only thing she can say. The tags stay on the umbrellas, and the next time a yellow one comes back, she lends it out again without asking a name.
- **｜七つの窓《ななつのまど》** — ｜坂井《さかい》 has kept the hut for thirty seasons because nothing down the mountain was waiting for him, and he wants the student to come back for the same unspoken reason. He never asks. He puts ｜湊《みなと》's cup back on the shelf for next summer instead, and when 湊 asks in the dark why he has stayed up here so long, he talks about the firewood. The night of the storm he does the one thing the job will not let him skip: he sits up feeding the fire two pieces an hour, the door at his back, the boy asleep two metres away, and the only boots the boy owns drying on the rack in plain sight. The snow stops at three and he checks the boots at ten past. So when the prints appear at dawn, nothing on the page lets them belong to 湊. They walk the evening round he forbade, clockwise under all seven windows and back to the door, never coming from the mountain or going to it, and every right print carries the half moon he cut crooked by accident and glued onto the boy's sole. The story does not say whose feet they were, and neither does he. What he does with them is the turn. A ｜小屋番《こやばん》 is someone whose round goes on being walked whether or not anyone is in the boots, and he has just seen what the job makes of a boy who stays. He shovels the prints away, his own along with them, and puts the cup in the bottom of the boy's rucksack. Thirty years earlier the ｜先代《せんだい》 went out alone on the last morning, came back, and packed 坂井's cup the same way with 「忘れ物じゃ」. The story sets that memory beside the prints and never connects them. It does not say what the old man shovelled that morning, and it remembers that 坂井 came back anyway and found the cup on the shelf. So the cup is not a refusal. It is the question asked the only way he can ask it, with the answer left in 湊's hands, and on the way down 湊 finds it, looks at him, and is told about the snow. The last image is the ordinary resuming: half moons going down the trail in fresh snow, a boy's prints now, and the keeper walking beside them rather than in them.
- **｜貸出中の本《かしだしちゅうのほん》** — A librarian refuses the first loan she ever wanted to make, and makes it a week later where no count can see it. ｜森《もり》 counts reading. Since spring she has wanted ｜樹《いつき》, the fourth grader who waits at the heater table every afternoon until his mother finishes work, to fill in the card form, and every time he has said 「別に、いい」. The story hands over the supernatural flatly and early, because the book is not the point. A children's novel comes back through the ｜返却《へんきゃく》ポスト with no barcode, and the card in its pocket carries a due date stamp, 57.1.5, January 1982, beside an empty space where the return should have been stamped. The bookmark inside, printed with the library's name from before the town's name changed, moves on every night in a building only she can unlock, and the story reports it as nothing but page numbers. Nobody explains it, and nobody is asked to. What the reader is not shown early is the scene with the angry mother, because it reads as a flaw. A book on reserve is two weeks overdue, and ｜森《もり》 will not call it in: 「｜貸出《かしだし》中の本は、｜返却《へんきゃく》されるまでお待ちください」. ｜小林《こばやし》 laughs at her for it, and it is the rule she keeps hardest. The boy starts reading the book because nobody pushed it at him, then races the bookmark, then asks who is ahead of him, and she offers to turn down the heater. On the last afternoon of the year he puts the signed form on the counter, she makes the card, and he asks for the book. The morning has already given the reader the numbers: 288, 258, and a book that ends at 300. She turns him down with her rule, and this time it protects a reader instead of an order. He spends the rest of the afternoon on the bench by the door, and the computer shows his new card with 0 loans. On January 5, the date on the old stamp, the bookmark lies behind the last page and stops. The paperweight from page four is the old date stamp, and she sets its wheels to 57.1.5 and stamps the return space, so the loan closes on time. When he comes back she writes his number under that line in pencil and stamps a due date by hand, which is the only record the book has. His first loan is real, and it never reaches the count she used to report. She does not take the old bookmark out.
- **｜調律の埃《ちょうりつのほこり》** — The dust check looks like a jealous craftsman's quirk, and then like a secret he shares with a seven-year-old. It is really the lock on the whole story. He taught the girl exactly how to tell whether another hand had been inside his piano, so she knew exactly how to hide hers. For twenty-five years, every summer holiday and every New Year, Chinatsu listened to how far his tuning had drifted, put it right, and then sifted dust from inside the case back onto the pins. Every clue is on the page, and each one had an explanation he liked better. The piano held its pitch through rainy seasons and winter heating, and he told other customers that his tunings held. The mother answered his boasting with the weather and the hydrangeas. Chinatsu came home exactly midway between his spring and autumn visits. A strip of red felt turned up where his is green, and the treble rang cleaner in autumn than he had left it in spring. He gives the reason for the refusal at eighteen as the work being no job for a girl, but the story shows the real one at seven: she heard the beats between two strings before he had named them, and he closed the lid early, the only time he ever left notes unchecked. Refusing her did not stop her becoming his apprentice. It made her a secret one, with no teacher to tell her when she was right, and it left him boasting of work that was half hers. Neither of them apologises or thanks the other. His whole concession is one sentence, that she need not put the dust back any more, and the hammer he leaves on the piano. The closing line, 千夏は、私の弟子だった, corrects his own refusal as a matter of fact, and the red felt is still in his bag among the green.
- **｜白い番号《しろいばんごう》** — The bicycle was never secondhand. ｜雄太《ゆうた》's father bought it new in March, in this shop near the station, on the morning he met his son for the first time in six years. He guessed the boy's height (｜百七十《ひゃくななじゅう》くらい) and guessed high, and he registered it in his own name because at that meeting the boy told him his new surname and would not tell him where he lived. Then nothing: the boy rode it every day and never called, and the father came back in the summer only to ask whether a boy had brought a black bicycle in. Everything the boy does is about keeping that one meeting from his mother. The 防犯登録 is what threatens it, because a policeman matched the bike to a name that was not his, and the rule the officer states on page three (first time a talk, second time a call home) is his deadline. The answer is on the page before the ledger opens: the 木 begun and struck out on the repair slip, the 雄 the boy shares with the buyer, a saddle set for someone taller, and a home number he will not give. The turn is the boy's. His father signs him free of the bike, writing 中村 without asking, which is the one sign the boy gets that the meeting was remembered; and once he is free of it he does not want to be, so he pockets the paper and keeps the bike in his father's name, knowing what the next stop will cost him at home. The shopkeeper's part is smaller and costs him more than he lets on. He phones a customer out of the 台帳, against his own rule and the boy's plea, in front of a wife who asked him to and is not pleased when he does. Then he covers his own number again and makes the only correction the ledger has ever had, and it is about a son rather than an owner.
- **いち、に、さん、し** — The radio was never leading. For ten years ｜石井《いしい》 has made it the authority over a circle of thirty: she sets the ｜音量《おんりょう》 at four, thinks of herself as only the person who carries it, and stands at the left end of the front row because that is where the knob is. All of it is arranged so that nothing depends on her. The complaint forces a silence she is sure will break the group, and it does break the front, which keeps time to the instructor's count. The one person it does not break is the one who never heard the radio at all. ｜田中《たなか》 is hard of hearing, and the story says so as a joke about answering the wrong question. He stands behind her because, he says, no wind reaches him there, and she files that with everything else he says. On the first silent morning she turns her arms the wrong way and he turns his the wrong way with her, exactly, while ｜木村《きむら》 whispers to him and he cannot hear. He has been following her back for ten years, including the morning the volume dropped and everyone else slipped and he did not. She does not turn round. She stops, he stops a beat later with his arms still raised, and she counts aloud for the first time in that park, loud enough for a deaf man, and her voice breaks on the last ｜四《し》. She gives up the radio without being asked, and the cost is that she can no longer be anyone's anonymous ｜当番《とうばん》. The last page is the weather. She moves from the left end to the middle and says the wind is strong today to the man who said no wind reaches him, and he agrees, and neither of them means the wind. The cicadas she could not hear on the first page are what the count is said over on the last.
