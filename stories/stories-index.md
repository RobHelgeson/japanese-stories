# 📚 Known-Word Stories

**This file is a build input.** `indexmd.py` reads the two `##` sections below and nothing else: `## Summaries` feeds each card's blurb and kana reading on the contents page, `## Afterwords` is folded into the reader and opened on the last page. One bullet per story in each, `- **｜題名《だいめい》** — text`, keyed on the title exactly as `corpus.json` spells it.

A story with no Summaries bullet still ships a card — `index.py` warns on stderr and leaves the blurb blank rather than stopping a rebuild that has already spent a minute per story in Ichiran. A missing Afterwords bullet is not checked at all.

Everything else that used to sit here was documentation of the reader, the build and the corpus, kept in parallel with the three files that own those subjects and wrong in all three by 2026-09-16: it described 傍点 as coloured underlines two designs out of date, counted five stories when there were eight, and quoted a known-set size 5 lemmas stale. Two-thirds of a build input, loaded as context beside the files it contradicted. It was deleted rather than corrected — see [README.md](../README.md) for the reader and the build, [AUTHORING.md](../AUTHORING.md) for the craft spec and the new-word rules, and [CALIBRATION.md](../CALIBRATION.md) for where the thresholds came from, which is where the two false claims this file used to record have gone.

## Summaries

Spoiler-light, for picking one.

- **｜時計の音《とけいのおと》** — Clearing out a grandfather's house, the narrator finds a watch that stopped years ago and starts again after midnight.
- **｜迷子の手紙《まいごのてがみ》** — A letter arrives at the right address under the wrong name, and answering it means telling a stranger something he does not know.
- **｜終電《しゅうでん》** — Two strangers on the last train, both leaving something behind, neither asking the other why.
- **｜猫を探す探偵《ねこをさがすたんてい》** — A cheap detective takes a lost-cat job and finds a child who has a reason.
- **｜城の鐘《しろのかね》** — A bell-founder's apprentice inherits the secret that the castle's war bell was built to fail, and the new king intends to ring it.
- **｜煙突の煙《えんとつのけむり》** — A woman clearing her late father's flat spends the last week of the neighbourhood bathhouse there, and works out why the water is still being heated after closing.
- **｜行かなかった人の地図《いかなかったひとのちず》** — A dying man asks his granddaughter to walk the road he spent fifty years drawing and never once travelled, and everything he drew on it turns out to be wrong.
- **｜廊下の鏡《ろうかのかがみ》** — Emptying the family house at seventy-one, a woman takes down the mirror her grandmother hung sixty years ago and never allowed anyone to move.

## Afterwords

Full spoilers. The reader keeps these folded away until the last page, and `index.py` does not put them on the contents page.

They exist because of a specific mistake. The 2026-09-08 prose pass cut the theme-announcing line from all five stories on the grounds that a narrator explaining the point is weaker than a scene that earns it. That is right for a native reader and wrong here: it removed the only handhold and left inference chains running thirty-plus sentences, in a reader that shows one sentence at a time with the English underneath. The fix is not to put ｜郷愁《きょうしゅう》というのは… back into the Japanese. It is to say it in English, afterwards, once the reading is done.

- **｜時計の音《とけいのおと》** — The grandfather's silence was never rejection, and three things say so without any of them saying it. The shop owner: 「｜何《なに》も｜買《か》わないで、｜店《みせ》の｜時計《とけい》の｜音《おと》を｜聞《き》いていた」. The letter: 「だから、｜直《なお》さなかった」. And the one time he was angry, 「｜静《しず》かにしろ。｜音《おと》が｜聞《き》こえない」. Read forward, that is a man telling a child to go away. Read back from the letter, it is a man asking the child to listen with him and failing to say so. The last page is the narrator finally doing what was asked: ｜時計《とけい》を｜耳《みみ》に｜当《あ》てて、｜目《め》を｜閉《と》じた. He hears it, and the story stops there rather than explaining it. The repair is where the watch goes afterwards — ｜部品《ぶひん》の｜箱《はこ》の｜隣《となり》ではなく、｜机《つくえ》の｜上《うえ》に｜置《お》いた — on the workbench, not in with the drawer of sorted parts. It is the one watch in that house his grandfather never fixed, and the grandson leaves it unfixed on purpose.
- **｜迷子の手紙《まいごのてがみ》** — Two stories, and the narrator only notices one. The visible one is the son's: a letter to a mother who died before it arrived, answered by a stranger, closed by a reply signed with a granddaughter's name the mother never knew. The invisible one is hers. The neighbour described ｜青山梓《あおやまあずさ》 as ｜毎日《まいにち》｜同《おな》じ｜時間《じかん》に｜買《か》い｜物《もの》へ｜出《で》かけて、｜誰《だれ》とも｜話《はな》さなかった. On the last page the narrator's own shopping hour has become the same every day, and the neighbour says 「｜松田《まつだ》さんも｜静《しず》かな｜人《ひと》ね」 — the same word she used for the dead woman. She moved into more than the house. Her one reach outward is the word ｜丁寧《ていねい》, which the neighbour never used and she added anyway: an invented kindness, for someone she will never meet.
- **｜終電《しゅうでん》** — About the conditions under which a person can finally say a true thing. He has told nobody that he quit, and the reason is given in the second page: ｜話《はな》す｜相手《あいて》がいないからだ. He says it to her — 「｜今日《きょう》までは」 — precisely because she boards the morning bullet train and he will never see her again. Her 「｜知《し》らない｜人《ひと》が｜多《おお》いほど、｜楽《らく》なんです」 is her reason for going to ｜東京《とうきょう》 and his reason for talking to her, and neither of them notices the overlap. She never asks what happened to him, and the narration says outright that this was the best thing about the night. They are carrying the same thing in different bags: hers holds her dead mother's ｜茶碗《ちゃわん》, wrapped in paper, everything else thrown away, and his holds the company papers he still cannot throw away. Her 「もう｜帰《かえ》る｜家《いえ》がないんです」 is left standing — he cannot answer it, and the story refuses to give him a line that would. What he does instead is the last page: the papers come out onto the desk, he turns the top sheet over, and the blank back is where he writes the names of towns he has never been to. The ｜網棚《あみだな》 over their heads is empty from the first exchange to the last line, because neither of them will put anything down. **A reading note:** 38 of the 39 quoted lines carry no attribution. Who is speaking comes from register alone — he asks, at length and over-politely, and she answers short. The narration hands you that key just before the longest exchange: ｜彼女《かのじょ》の｜返事《へんじ》はいつも｜短《みじか》い。｜私《わたし》の｜質問《しつもん》は、どうしても｜長《なが》くなる。
- **｜猫を探す探偵《ねこをさがすたんてい》** — The case closes on page 21 and the story runs to thirty, which is the design: finding the cat, the boy and the reason takes five days, and the rest is deciding what to do about it. Everything the reader needs is in plain view early. The boxes outside a house on page 8 are a family packing to move, and the detective walks straight past them. The boy names the white ear-tip without once looking at the photograph — 「その｜猫《ねこ》、｜耳《みみ》の｜先《さき》が｜白《しろ》いよ」 — and the line the detective has already heard twice is that only an owner remembers the tip of an ear. The payoff is him putting those two facts together on page 21, one page after the boy says 「｜来週《らいしゅう》、｜引《ひ》っ｜越《こ》すんだ」. What the story is actually about is the second plant, and it is about the detective rather than the boy. He does not open the warehouse door when he could — ｜大人《おとな》が｜扉《とびら》を｜開《あ》ければ、その｜子《こ》は｜次《つぎ》の｜場所《ばしょ》を｜探《さが》すだけだ — and he promises only the one thing he can keep, 「｜君《きみ》が｜盗《ぬす》んだとは｜言《い》わない」, while fully intending to tell ｜松田《まつだ》 everything. That is not kindness and the story never calls it kindness. It is a man who borrowed a book at twelve, never gave it back, can no longer remember whose it was, and still has it on his office shelf. ｜松田《まつだ》 then gives up something she is entitled to keep: she says 「｜私《わたし》の｜猫《ねこ》です」 once and never says it again. The old photograph on her shelf, the cat beside a man the detective does not ask about, is the only evidence of what the animal is worth to her, and she does not explain that either. Three people hand something over and none of them announce it. The last page is deliberately flat — the office is cold, the book is exactly where it has always been, and half a cat-finding fee does not cover the rent.
- **｜城の鐘《しろのかね》** — The master's crime is the plot; the inheritance is the story. Forty years earlier he cast the war bell thin at the top and thick at the bottom so it would crack under a hard strike, and told no one until he was dying. He calls it guilt: 「これは｜罪《つみ》だ」, 「わしは｜裏切《うらぎ》り｜者《もの》だ」. The apprentice takes the same act onto himself on the last page and then refuses the word for it — ｜私《わたし》は｜師匠《ししょう》の｜罪《つみ》を、｜自分《じぶん》のものとして｜引《ひ》き｜受《う》けた。けれど、｜罪《つみ》だとはもう｜思《おも》っていない — which is the one thing in the story that changes. Everything hangs on a distinction stated once, in dialogue on page 12: あの｜鐘《かね》は、｜強《つよ》く｜打《う》てば｜必《かなら》ず｜割《わ》れる。｜弱《よわ》く｜打《う》てば、｜良《よ》い｜音《おと》がする. Every scene after it is somebody deciding how hard to strike, and the two men who could have stopped it both decline to ask the question that would have told them. The captain asks 「｜鐘《かね》は｜打《う》てる｜状態《じょうたい》か」 when he could have asked whether it would crack; when he finally does ask that, he has come down to the cell without a key on him. The closing image repeats the opening exactly: a twelve-year-old with no parents, striking a small bell hard, and not being stopped.

- **｜煙突の煙《えんとつのけむり》** — The owner has been heating the water past closing for three months, waiting for a regular who came late from the factory. That regular was her father, and nobody ever told him. She has not told him either, and she has spent the whole week clearing the flat without telling anyone in the neighbourhood she was there. The chimney is how these people used to pass news without speaking: as a child she knew her father was on his way home when the smoke went out, and it is the same smoke still going up an hour past the eleven o'clock on the notice that makes her ask. The answer is sitting in plain view from page 7: every ｜桶《おけ》 on the shelf is upended on its base, and one is left standing mouth-up, kept aside for a man who is not coming. When she finally tells him on the last night, the owner does not speak at all. He climbs down from the ｜番台《ばんだい》, lifts that ｜桶《おけ》 off the shelf in both hands and fills it, which is the first thing in the story to be finished rather than kept waiting. She sets her father's worn-down soap on the ｜番台《ばんだい》. Two pages later the ｜桶《おけ》 has been turned over and stacked in with all the others, and nothing is said about that either. The closing image is the smoke gone before ten, an hour earlier than the notice allows, seen from the window she watched it from as a child, and this time it means what it always meant.

- **｜行かなかった人の地図《いかなかったひとのちず》** — The map is not wrong. It is early. The bridge ｜梓《あずさ》 cannot find fell seventy-five years ago, the year of the great snow, five years before her grandfather was born, which means he never saw it either. He was not drawing the road. He was drawing his father's journey, exactly as it was told to him, and a told journey does not update. The small ｜印《しるし》 beside each landmark, which she put down to an old man's habit as a child and never once asked about, are counts: one for every time his father told him of a thing. Four beside the bridge, seven beside the village, twelve beside the split rock, sixty-three beside the tree at the end, which is why the tree is drawn far too large and why none of the distances are true. The map is drawn to the scale of what was remembered rather than what was walked, and that is how it fails at every particular and still arrives. The ｜主《ぬし》 on the ｜峠《とうげ》 asks the question the whole story is made of, and ｜梓《あずさ》's first answer is refused because it is her grandfather's sentence and not hers. The one thing on the paper he could not get right is the sea, a flat blue line, because the sea is the one thing his father had words for and no way to show him. It is also the only thing he ever wanted. He does not ask her about the tree when she comes home.

- **｜廊下の鏡《ろうかのかがみ》** — The mirror was never for looking into. The grandmother hung it at the turn of the hallway, set at an angle to the one window at the front of the house, so that the light would go around the corner into the back rooms that have no window of their own. She never said so. She said only that it was not to be moved, and she polished it far more often than a mirror anyone looked into would need, because dust costs light. Her daughter inherited the instruction without the reason, kept the angle, kept polishing, and treated both as a rule rather than as a mechanism, which is why she wrapped the glass herself at the roof repair and stood over the workmen until the nail went back in the same hole. Every clue is in plain view from the first page. It hangs where nobody dresses. It faces a window rather than a room. It is cleaned on bright days and left alone on dull ones. She is twice shown polishing it and never once shown looking at her own reflection, which is what the 一方で sentence on page nine is for. And the child never needs a lamp at the turn of the hallway, which she reports as a fact about her childhood rather than as a fact about the mirror. The turn is an event and not a realization: she lifts the mirror off the nail to wrap it, and the back of the hallway goes dark around her while she is standing in it holding the thing that was lighting it. She puts it back three times, and by the third she is counting years instead of checking. Thirty of them, none of it ever explained to anyone. The last line corrects a sentence the reader was handed on page ten in her parents' voices, 「おばあさんは、不思議なことを信じる人だから」, and it is a correction about a person rather than a statement about the story.
