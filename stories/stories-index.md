# 📚 Known-Word Stories

**This file is a build input.** `indexmd.py` reads the two `## ` sections below and nothing else: `## Summaries` feeds each card's blurb and kana reading on the contents page, `## Afterwords` is folded into the reader and opened on the last page. One bullet per story in each, `- **｜題名《だいめい》** — text`, keyed on the title exactly as `corpus.json` spells it.

A story with no Summaries bullet still ships a card — `index.py` warns on stderr and leaves the blurb blank rather than stopping a rebuild that has already spent a minute per story in Ichiran. A missing Afterwords bullet is not checked at all.

Everything else that used to sit here was documentation of the reader, the build and the corpus, kept in parallel with the three files that own those subjects and wrong in all three by 2026-09-16: it described 傍点 as coloured underlines two designs out of date, counted five stories when there were eight, and quoted a known-set size 5 lemmas stale. Two-thirds of a build input, loaded as context beside the files it contradicted. It was deleted rather than corrected — see [README.md](../README.md) for the reader and the build, [AUTHORING.md](../AUTHORING.md) for the craft spec and the new-word rules, and [CALIBRATION.md](../CALIBRATION.md) for where the thresholds came from, which is where the two false claims this file used to record have gone.

## Summaries

Spoiler-light, for picking one.

- **｜時計の音《とけいのおと》** — Clearing out a grandfather's house, the narrator finds a watch that stopped years ago and starts again after midnight.
- **｜迷子の手紙《まいごのてがみ》** — A letter arrives at the right address under the wrong name, and answering it means telling a stranger something he does not know.
- **｜終電《しゅうでん》** — Two strangers on the last train, both leaving something behind, neither asking the other why.
- **｜猫を探す探偵《ねこをさがすたんてい》** — A cheap detective takes a lost-cat job and finds a child who has a reason.
- **｜城の鐘《しろのかね》** — A bell-founder's apprentice inherits the secret that the castle's war bell was built to fail, and the new king intends to ring it.
- **｜煙突の煙《えんとつのけむり》** — A woman clearing her late father's flat spends the last week of the neighbourhood bathhouse there, and works out why the water is still being heated after closing.
- **｜行かなかった人の地図《いかなかったひとのちず》** — A dying man asks his granddaughter to walk the road he spent fifty years drawing and never once travelled, and everything he drew on it turns out to be wrong.
- **｜廊下の鏡《ろうかのかがみ》** — Emptying the family house at seventy-one, a woman takes down the mirror her grandmother hung sixty years ago and never allowed anyone to move.

## Afterwords

Full spoilers. The reader keeps these folded away until the last page, and `index.py` does not put them on the contents page.

They exist because of a specific mistake. The 2026-09-08 prose pass cut the theme-announcing line from all five stories on the grounds that a narrator explaining the point is weaker than a scene that earns it. That is right for a native reader and wrong here: it removed the only handhold and left inference chains running thirty-plus sentences, in a reader that shows one sentence at a time with the English underneath. The fix is not to put ｜郷愁《きょうしゅう》というのは… back into the Japanese. It is to say it in English, afterwards, once the reading is done.

- **｜時計の音《とけいのおと》** — The grandfather's silence was never rejection, and three things say so without any of them saying it. The shop owner: 「｜何《なに》も｜買《か》わないで、｜店《みせ》の｜時計《とけい》の｜音《おと》を｜聞《き》いていた」. The letter: 「だから、｜直《なお》さなかった」. And the one time he was angry, 「｜静《しず》かにしろ。｜音《おと》が｜聞《き》こえない」. Read forward, that is a man telling a child to go away. Read back from the letter, it is a man asking the child to listen with him and failing to say so. The last page is the narrator finally doing what was asked: ｜時計《とけい》を｜耳《みみ》に｜当《あ》てて、｜目《め》を｜閉《と》じた. He hears it. Promising to tell his own grandchild is the repair, a generation late.
- **｜迷子の手紙《まいごのてがみ》** — Two stories, and the narrator only notices one. The visible one is the son's: a letter to a mother who died before it arrived, answered by a stranger, closed by a reply signed with a granddaughter's name the mother never knew. The invisible one is hers. The neighbour described ｜青山梓《あおやまあずさ》 as ｜毎日《まいにち》｜同《おな》じ｜時間《じかん》に｜買《か》い｜物《もの》へ｜出《で》かけて、｜誰《だれ》とも｜話《はな》さなかった. On the last page the narrator's own shopping hour has become the same every day, and the neighbour says 「あなたも｜静《しず》かな｜人《ひと》ね」. She moved into more than the apartment. Her one reach outward is the word ｜丁寧《ていねい》, which the neighbour never used and she added anyway: an invented kindness, for someone she will never meet.
- **｜終電《しゅうでん》** — About the conditions under which a person can finally say a true thing. He tells a stranger he has lost his job precisely because she gets off at the next stop. Her 「｜知《し》らない｜人《ひと》が｜多《おお》いほど、｜楽《らく》なんです」 is her reason for moving to Tokyo and his reason for talking to her, and neither of them notices the overlap. Then she says 「もう｜帰《かえ》る｜家《いえ》がないんです」 about herself, and by the last page he has quietly taken it for his own: ｜帰《かえ》る｜家《いえ》がないということは、どこへでも｜行《い》けるということだ. He admits she did not mean it that way. That is the claim, and he states it outright: でも｜言葉《ことば》は、｜聞《き》いた｜人《ひと》の｜物《もの》になる. The final image earns it without comment. The company papers have been sitting on his desk the whole story as the dead weight of the old job; he turns the top sheet over and writes the names of towns he has never been to on the blank back. **A reading note:** almost none of the dialogue is attributed. Who is speaking comes from register alone — his lines run long and over-polite, hers are four to eight characters. The narration hands you that key just before the longest exchange.
- **｜猫を探す探偵《ねこをさがすたんてい》** — The case is solved by page 20 and the story keeps going, which is the design. Finding the cat, the boy and the reason takes a week; the rest is deciding what to do about it. He waits instead of opening the warehouse door (｜大人《おとな》が｜子供《こども》の｜隠《かく》れる｜場所《ばしょ》を｜開《あ》けるのは｜簡単《かんたん》すぎる), takes half the fee, and ｜松田《まつだ》 declines to press a claim she is entitled to. The photograph on the shelf, the cat beside a man the detective never identifies, is the only evidence of why it matters to her, and she never explains. Unusually for this set, the last two lines state the thesis outright rather than leaving it: ｜探偵《たんてい》の｜仕事《しごと》は｜真実《しんじつ》を｜見《み》つけることじゃない。｜見《み》つけた｜真実《しんじつ》をどうするかだ。
- **｜城の鐘《しろのかね》** — The master's crime is the plot; the inheritance is the story. Forty years earlier he cast the war bell thin at the top and thick at the bottom so it would crack under a hard strike, and told no one until he was dying. He calls it guilt: 「これは｜罪《つみ》だ」「｜私《わたし》は｜裏切《うらぎ》り｜者《もの》だ」. The apprentice reframes it on the last page as ｜選《えら》ぶ｜権利《けんり》, the right to choose. Everything hangs on one distinction stated once, in dialogue on page 11: ｜強《つよ》く｜打《う》てば｜必《かなら》ず｜割《わ》れる、｜弱《よわ》く｜打《う》てば｜良《よ》い｜音《おと》がする. Every scene after it is somebody deciding how hard to strike. The closing image repeats the opening exactly: a twelve-year-old with no parents, striking a small bell hard, and not being stopped.

- **｜煙突の煙《えんとつのけむり》** — The owner has been heating the water past closing for three months, waiting for a regular who came late from the factory. That regular was her father, and nobody ever told him. She has not told him either, and she has spent the whole week clearing the flat without telling anyone in the neighbourhood she was there. The chimney is how these people used to pass news without speaking: as a child she knew her father was on his way home when the smoke went out, and the same smoke going up after nine is what makes her ask. The upside-down ｜桶《おけ》 on the shelf is the answer sitting in plain view from page 7, kept aside for a man who is not coming. When she finally says it on the last night, the owner does not speak at all. He climbs down, turns the ｜桶《おけ》 the right way up and fills it, which is the first time anything in the story is put right rather than kept waiting. She sets her father's worn-down soap on the ｜番台《ばんだい》 beside it. The closing image is the smoke gone at nine for the first time, seen from the window she watched it from as a child, and this time it means the same thing it always did.

- **｜行かなかった人の地図《いかなかったひとのちず》** — The map is not wrong. It is early. The bridge ｜梓《あずさ》 cannot find fell seventy-five years ago, the year of the great snow, five years before her grandfather was born, which means he never saw it either. He was not drawing the road. He was drawing his father's journey, exactly as it was told to him, and a told journey does not update. The small ｜印《しるし》 beside each landmark, which she has taken for decoration her whole life, are counts: one for every time his father mentioned a thing. Four beside the bridge, seven beside the village, sixty-three beside the tree at the end, which is why the tree is drawn far too large and why none of the distances are true. The map is drawn to the scale of what was remembered rather than what was walked, and that is how it fails at every particular and still arrives. The ｜主《ぬし》 on the ｜峠《とうげ》 asks the question the whole story is made of, and ｜梓《あずさ》's first answer is refused because it is her grandfather's sentence and not hers. The one thing on the paper he could not get right is the sea, a flat blue line, because the sea is the one thing his father had words for and no way to show him. It is also the only thing he ever wanted. He does not ask her about the tree when she comes home.

- **｜廊下の鏡《ろうかのかがみ》** — The mirror was never for looking into. The grandmother hung it at the turn of the hallway, set at an angle to the one window at the front of the house, so that the light would go around the corner into the back rooms that have no window of their own. She never said so. She said only that it was not to be moved, and she polished it far more often than a mirror anyone looked into would need, because dust costs light. Her daughter inherited the instruction without the reason, kept the angle, kept polishing, and treated both as a rule rather than as a mechanism, which is why she wrapped the glass herself at the roof repair and stood over the workmen until the nail went back in the same hole. Every clue is in plain view from the first page. It hangs where nobody dresses. It faces a window rather than a room. It is cleaned on bright days and left alone on dull ones. She is twice shown polishing it and never once shown looking at her own reflection, which is what the 一方で sentence on page nine is for. And the child never needs a lamp at the turn of the hallway, which she reports as a fact about her childhood rather than as a fact about the mirror. The turn is an event and not a realization: she lifts the mirror off the nail to wrap it, and the back of the hallway goes dark around her while she is standing in it holding the thing that was lighting it. She puts it back three times, and by the third she is counting years instead of checking. Thirty of them, none of it ever explained to anyone. The last line corrects a sentence the reader was handed on page ten in her parents' voices, 「おばあさんは、不思議なことを信じる人だから」, and it is a correction about a person rather than a statement about the story.
