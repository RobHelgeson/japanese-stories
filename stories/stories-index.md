They exist because of a specific mistake. The 2026-09-08 prose pass cut the theme-announcing line from all five stories on the grounds that a narrator explaining the point is weaker than a scene that earns it. That is right for a native reader and wrong here: it removed the only handhold and left inference chains running thirty-plus sentences, in a reader that shows one sentence at a time with the English underneath. - **｜一人半《ひとりはん》** — ｜文子《ふみこ》 wanted to be counted, and found out she had already been paid for. She is fourteen in June 1955, in a rice hamlet in the hills of ｜安曇野《あづみの》. A house in debt asks for nothing. It is put last of the five, with the oldest seedlings, and does not ask to go first, and the mother says they can ask nobody for anything until their day is done. ｜文子《ふみこ》 is a child, and the exchange's book (｜帳面《ちょうめん》) writes only grown hands. Her want is to go all four days as the house's fourth hand and be written as a full hand (｜一人前《いちにんまえ》), because by her own sum that clears the debt. Her need is to see that her house wants her for more than her hands. She cannot see it yet, so she reads every change in the house, the bride above all, as someone taking her place in the line, and she spends two months resenting a woman who was paying to take her out of the line. The twist is this. In May the ｜中学《ちゅうがく》 teacher came to the house, and ｜文子《ふみこ》 was sent out to the seedbed (｜苗代《なわしろ》) while the adults talked. She took the visit for a complaint about her absences, which were real: the week after her father went down, and the day the seedbed was made. Coming back to the door she heard her father say that a house in debt cannot leave a pair of hands sitting idle, and she was glad, because she took it to mean he counted her as one. The visit was about the ｜高校《こうこう》 in town, and the father had refused it for exactly that reason: three years of her hands idle in a classroom while the lane carried the house. ｜雪子《ゆきこ》 bowed to him and offered her own hands. On the 17th ｜文子《ふみこ》 walks into a line whose hills have been shared, ｜糸《いと》 will not share them twice against the host's word that its daughter is the kitchen, and the bride steps out and gives her her six. The bride of a house in debt not planting her own house's paddy would be the talk of the village by night, so her brother ｜実《みのる》 says it in one breath with the rope in his hand: the school, the father's words about idle hands, ｜雪子《ゆきこ》's promise, and that nobody told ｜文子《ふみこ》 because they would not have her owing her brother's bride. ｜糸《いと》 says the count at the evening meal. Twelve came today, eleven women and ｜金蔵《きんぞう》, which with the three spring days makes fifteen taken. Given back: ｜実《みのる》 four, ナツ four, ｜雪子《ゆきこ》 six, one and a half on each of the four days, her halves paying back two of the three. - **｜拍子木《ひょうしぎ》** — The clappers' counts are explained in the story; the snow is not. The far bank of the river held a hamlet of eleven houses with its own section (｜班《はん》) of the brigade until two springs after the snow year, the story's version of the great snow of 1963. Then ten of the households left town, and ｜丸山《まるやま》 alone did not: he took his house apart, carried it over the bridge and built it again, and it is the last house on this side's round. That is a documented kind of leaving in snow country, not a death; the people are alive somewhere, and the story never mourns them. Ten roofs are left over there, seven fallen in and three standing. What the story keeps back is what ｜丸山《まるやま》's three strikes at the bridge are. The reader is let assume, with ｜和夫《かずお》, that they are the last word of a far-bank man to his own roofs. They are not. In the snow year the far road was under snow for eight days, the bridge was snowed shut, the telephone line was down, and on a snowy night a voice does not carry over the river, only the clappers. Every night three strikes came from the far end of the bridge and this side struck two back, and that was how the hut knew the eleven houses were alive. Three was the far hut's door count and two is this hut's. The three were ｜丸山《まるやま》's, as head of the far section; the two were ｜本間《ほんま》's. ｜丸山《まるやま》 has struck the three at the bridge on every night of the year-end watch since he came across, and nobody has answered, because the clappers on any round he walks are his, because the last newest man to strike the year's last round did it the winter before ｜丸山《まるやま》 came across (「｜十年《じゅうねん》ぶりじゃ」), the brigade having taken in nobody since, and because ｜本間《ほんま》's knees keep him in the hut. On the last night the hut's custom puts the clappers in ｜和夫《かずお》's hand. At the bridge he hands them back, and ｜丸山《まるやま》 strikes his three, holds them out again and says 「｜二《ふた》つ｜打《う》て」. ｜和夫《かずお》 says the thing he swallowed on the twenty-eighth, that there is nobody over there, and behind it, in no order, that he is quitting in March. ｜丸山《まるやま》 says 「そうか」, raises the clappers toward him and says 「｜二《ふた》つだ」. ｜和夫《かずお》 strikes two, and the second comes late because his hands are cold. 「｜遅《おそ》い」 is about that second strike. It is the only thing ｜丸山《まるやま》 says about the two, and it is what he would say to a junior who struck badly at the hut door: he takes the two as a real answer and corrects its timing. Beyond 「そうか」, it is also all he says to the quit and to the verdict. The next morning ｜本間《ほんま》, who has already heard the quit from ｜丸山《まるやま》, writes ｜和夫《かずお》's name at the very end of the line for the ceremony on the sixth, and then tells the nights, the part of the snow story ｜丸山《まるやま》 cuts off every year, and ｜和夫《かずお》 learns what he struck. He says nothing. On the twenty-eighth he kept back from ｜丸山《まるやま》 that the round is walked for nobody. On the first he keeps back from ｜本間《ほんま》 that the two he struck at the bridge the night before answered ｜丸山《まるやま》's three, the first answer in nine years, because what he did on a senior's round at a senior's word is the senior's to tell. The snow is the one thing the story never explains. While they stood on the bridge, snow fell on the far bank, on the road up the slope and on the ten roofs, straight and thick, out of a sky that over the bridge and the town was clear to the stars, and the bridge under ｜和夫《かずお》's glove was dry. It was already falling before anything was struck, and it was still falling, only over there, when ｜和夫《かずお》 looked back. ｜丸山《まるやま》 stood with the snowing slope at his back. Nobody speaks of it; ｜和夫《かずお》's 「｜雪《ゆき》が……」 is answered with the count. What it is: the snow year, come back over the far bank for one night. The three strikes were made for a bank buried in snow, and on the one night in nine years when someone on this side holds the clappers and can answer them, the bank is buried again, as it was on the eight nights when an answer meant the houses were alive. There is nobody under it now, so ｜和夫《かずお》's verdict is true. The theme is what a round is for. ｜和夫《かずお》 has decided the town is finished and the round is walked for nobody, and he counts the dark windows as evidence, so that leaving will cost him nothing and need nobody's leave. On the twenty-eighth more than twenty windows are dark, in a town where houses with only the old left in them put their lights out early; on the thirty-first all thirty-two doors have their New Year pines up. A dark window at that hour is an old household asleep, and a pine at the door is a household keeping the New Year, so his own two counts say that every house on this side is lived in: the dark windows were evidence that the town is old, never that it is finished. The verdict has never been tested, because the one man it could be put to is the man whose round it is, and a first-year does not correct a senior's round. The pressure that keeps it unsaid is 先輩・後輩, and the same pressure keeps the quit unsaid: a junior who opens his mouth on a senior's round is answered with an instruction before he gets to the end. To get the quit out at the bridge, ｜和夫《かずお》 has to say the verdict first, to the man's face, to the one man in the town who was born on the empty side. ｜丸山《まるやま》 does not deny it. He does not say there is somebody, and he stands with his back to the far bank. The three were always a call to this side, asking for its two; for nine years he has been calling the town, not the empty bank. The moral, stated here and nowhere in the story: a signal is kept alive by whoever answers it, not by whoever sends it, and a town is not finished when its far bank empties but when nobody on its own side has the clappers in hand to strike two. He still leaves in March, and the story does not keep him. His word goes up through ｜丸山《まるやま》 to ｜本間《ほんま》 without him, his name goes on the list for the sixth at the very end of the line, and the last thing said to him is where to walk. Why it is built this way. The thirty-two houses, the door strikes, the clear sky with its stars and the dry bridge under the glove are all ordinary and countable, and the snow is the one thing on the bridge that is wrong. The anchor is the clappers and their counts: two at a house, two at the door, three at the bridge, and on the last night two at the bridge. They carry the new words 拍子木, 消防団, 詰所 and 懐中電灯, handled in every scene, with 火の用心 called before the strikes. The three strikes at the bridge are first shown as an old man's habit at the end of the round and left to settle into the obvious reading. The two strikes at the door are shown as hut procedure, answered without looking up. ｜本間《ほんま》's snow story is an old man's annual story that ｜丸山《まるやま》 cuts off at the same line every year, and the line he cuts is the nights. 「｜久《ひさ》しぶりだな、｜一番《いちばん》｜下《した》が｜打《う》つのは」 on the twenty-eighth is banter about a custom gathering dust, and it is also the fact that nobody but ｜丸山《まるやま》 has held the clappers at the bridge for nine years. ｜本間《ほんま》 never leaving the hut is what an old head with bad knees does, and it is why nobody has answered. ｜丸山《まるやま》's 「｜後《うし》ろを｜歩《ある》くだけだ」 in April is what a recruiter says to make the job sound small; the one night ｜和夫《かずお》 walks in front is the night he is told to answer, and the last line, 「｜六日《むいか》は｜後《うし》ろを｜歩《ある》け」, puts him behind again. The custom that the newest man strikes the year's last round is stated on the twenty-eighth as procedure, so the handover on the thirty-first is the hut doing what it said it would, not a gift. What ｜丸山《まるやま》 adds at the bridge, 二つ打て, is his own, and it is an order because a senior cannot ask a junior for anything. ｜丸山《まるやま》 being from the far bank is said plainly and early, as a thing the whole town knows, so that the story is not a puzzle about a man's birthplace and the reader knows, as ｜和夫《かずお》 does, whom the verdict is said to. ｜本間《ほんま》 is the head of this hut; the town's brigade chief is another man, who came round on the thirtieth and left the sake. The story has two nights, a remembered April and one morning. The father once quit the brigade himself. On the thirty-first, sweeping the snow at the gate, he tells ｜和夫《かずお》 that he said one word to ｜丸山《まるやま》 and ｜丸山《まるやま》 went to ｜本間《ほんま》, which is why the family knows the order of telling, and it is the order the morning follows. The senior is not a mentor and hands nothing on.
